## Mapping global spatiotemporal patterns in male sperm whale presence through passive acoustic monitoring

### Context and Approach

Sperm whales are considered vulnerable by the International Union for Conservation of Nature, but many aspects of their demographics, movements and habitat use, which are key to effective conservation and management of any at-risk wildlife species, remain poorly described. This is especially true for mature males who are generally solitary, travel vast distances, and segregate spatially and socially from groups of females. 

To address these gaps, we have compiled an extensive collection of acoustic datasets, collected across the Eastern Tropical Pacific, Northeast Pacific, North Atlantic and Arctic Oceans. In these datasets, we are harnessing sperm whales’ nearly continuously produced echolocation clicks, which also encode information about body size, and by extension, age-sex class (due to sexual size dimorphism). Using machine-learning based software (Beslin *et al.* 2018), we are efficiently and automatically detecting sperm whales and determining body size and age-sex class distributions from their clicks. By comparing these distributions across locations, years and seasons, we are assessing when, where, and with whom sperm whales of different age-sex classes are found globally, with a focus on mature males.
